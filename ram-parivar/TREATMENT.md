# Shri Ram Parivar — treatment

Style: Gilded Illumination (`gilded-illumination`). 6.0 s, 1920×1080, 30 fps (the style's rate). Music and foley, no voice, no subtitles. One inscription: "SHRI RAM PARIVAR".

## Three candidate structures

1. **Continuous narration**: Rama, Sita and Lakshmana walk through a forest, Hanuman joins, they arrive at a throne. Too many stations for 6 s; each would flash past.
2. **Into the material**: pull out of a macro of leaf into the family. That's the demo's opening; skip it.
3. **Darshan: gold laid, then lit** (chosen). A dark gold ground and one small diya flame. The family is painted in layers, the flame touches Rama's halo, and the gold lights in rings. One image, one turn (dark → lit), which is how a devotional picture is "seen": the lamp is raised and the deity appears. It fits 6 s because it is a single picture.

## Logline and arc

A single lamp is lit before an unlit gold ground; the Ram Parivar is painted into it, and when the flame touches Rama's halo every piece of gold awakens.

- Setup (0–0.7 s): dark leaf, one flame.
- Build (0.7–2.7 s): the family painted in layers, camera pulling back.
- Breath (2.7–3.3 s): silence, the inscription written.
- Turn (3.33 s): the touch; gold lights in rings.
- Ending (4.7–6 s): a band of light rakes across the finished picture.

## Benchmark

- Tanjore and Mysore painting (learn: gold-relief halos and arches, frontal seated deities, symmetric darshan composition, Hanuman kneeling at the feet; don't take: any specific painting's figures or layout).
- Pre-dawn aarti (learn: one flame revealing gold in the dark, a bell at the moment of light; don't take: any recorded chant or melody).
